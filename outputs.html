    Output fields¶

    This page defines every output file of the two solvers: what it holds, its sign convention, units, reference time and frame. All statements are taken from the code that writes the files (python/slevolve/solver.py, python/run_ais.py, python/run_outer.py, and the output section of sl_solver / sl_solver_init in fortran/src/sl_model_mod.f90). The binary containers are specified in File formats.

    1. Conventions common to all fields¶

    Grid layout. Every grid file is a (512, 1024) array a[j, i]. Row j is the Gauss-Legendre latitude GLlat[j], running from north (+89.73 deg) to south; column i is longitude i * 360/1024 degrees east (0 to 359.65). Longitude varies fastest in memory. tools/make_grid.py writes the latitudes and longitudes as text (Input files).

    Step index. File index N is the step number. Ice file <icemodel>N is the ice thickness at the end of step N, i.e. at time N * dt after the start (with a times file: at time t_N, line N + 1 of the file; Input files); ice file 0 is the initial state. The step numbers in the output names are the same with and without a times file.

    • N = 0: the initial state, written before the first step (tgrid0, ocean0, beta0, and in Fortran dS_converged0 = 0 and TPW0 = 0).
    • N = 1: the state after the first step (time dt, or t_1 with a times file), computed with ice file 1.
    • N = k: the state after step k (time k * dt, or t_k).

    Reference time. tgrid, ocean and beta are absolute (the state at the end of step N). The changes R, G and dS_converged are cumulative since step 0 (the start of the run, or of the outer pass), not increments of one step.

    Sign of topography and sea level. The main variable is the topography T: the elevation of the solid surface (bedrock, without the ice) relative to the sea surface, in metres, positive up. The ocean is where T < 0. The solver updates it as T_N = T_0 - dSL_N, where dSL_N is the sea-level change since step 0 (positive = sea-level rise). So the relative sea-level change since step 0 is -(tgrid<N> - tgrid0).

    Degree-1 frame. R and G contain degree 1 in the frame of the Love-number file; SLEvolve applies no frame conversion. The shipped Love-number files are in the CE frame (k_1 = 0). The Case 1 file is CE in its elastic and fluid limits but carries a degree-1 initialisation defect in its residues, which changes degree-1 R and G but not sea level or topography (lovenum/DEGREE1_NOTES.md).

    Masks. ocean and beta are 0/1 fields, stored as <f8 (Python --io-compat legacy: uint8).

    2. Grid and spectral fields (per step N)¶

    file meaning units sign / reference
    tgrid<N> topography T at the end of step N: solid surface (bedrock) minus sea surface. tgrid0 is the initial topography of the run (or of the outer pass) m positive up (land), negative in the ocean; absolute
    ocean<N> ocean function C: 1 where tgrid<N> < 0, else 0. It does not look at the ice: marine cells under grounded ice have C = 1. The ocean that carries water load is C * beta 0/1 absolute
    beta<N> grounded-ice mask: 1 where the grounded ice of step N (after the marine check, flotation on the topography of the last inner re-guess) is zero (below machine epsilon), else 0, so the ocean under floating shelves has beta = 1. beta0 is the same mask for ice file 0 on the initial topography 0/1 absolute
    R<N> radial displacement of the solid surface since step 0, including the rotational term m positive up; cumulative since step 0; degree 1 in the Love-number frame
    G<N> change of the sea surface (geoid plus the spatially uniform term from mass conservation) since step 0, G = dSL + R m positive up; cumulative since step 0; degree 1 in the Love-number frame
    dS_converged<N> spherical-harmonic coefficients of the change of ocean (water) thickness since step 0, after the inner iteration of step N has converged. The water load is rhow * dS m of water cumulative since step 0
    horiz<N>, Psi<N>, utheta<N>, uphi<N> optional horizontal displacement, see Horizontal displacement m since step 0
    dSLdt<N>, dRdt<N> optional analytic rates of sea level and radial displacement at the end of step N, see below m/yr instantaneous rate at t_N

    The sea-level change itself, dSL = G - R, is not written as a separate file; it equals tgrid0 - tgrid<N>.

    Spectral convention of dS_converged (and horiz). Complex (L+1, L+1) array a[l, m], 0 <= m <= l, zero above the diagonal, in the convention of the legacy spharmt module (python/slevolve/sht.py): a[0, 0] is the global mean of the field (to a relative 1.7e-9, from the truncated 1.41421356 literal of the legacy source), and the field is f = sum_l [ a[l,0] c_l0(mu) + 2 Re sum_{m>=1} a[l,m] c_lm(mu) exp(i m phi) ] with c_lm = 1.41421356 * P~_lm, P~_lm orthonormal on [-1, 1] without the Condon-Shortley phase. python/slevolve/sht.py (SHT.synthesis) and sht_ducc.py synthesise it.

    Analytic rates (optional)¶

    Off by default; Fortran SLEVOLVE_RATES=1, Python run_ais.py --save-fields ...,dSLdt,dRdt or run_outer.py --rates. Output only: every other output is unchanged. The formulas are in Method.

    file meaning units
    dSLdt<N> (.npy grid) rate of the sea-level change dSL (sea surface minus solid surface) at the end of step N, including the rotational term and, in degree 0, the rate of the conservation term on the ocean function of step N. -dSLdt is the rate of topography change m/yr
    dRdt<N> (.npy grid) rate of the radial displacement (uplift rate), including the rotational term m/yr
    TPWrate<N> (Fortran, text) / TPWrate<N>.txt (Python) with rotation: the rates of the three records of TPW<N>, in the same layout: il-dot (9 values, kg m^2/yr), m-dot (3 values, 1/yr; m1-dot, m2-dot is the polar-motion (true polar wander) rate in rad/yr, toward 0 E and 90 E; direction atan2(m2-dot, m1-dot) in degrees east), Lambda-dot (18 values, m^2 s^-2 /yr) as stated

    The rate of the sea surface is dGdt = dSLdt + dRdt. Present-day J2-dot and the true-polar-wander rate follow from these: with \(\dot G_{20}\) the (2,0) coefficient of dGdt (spharmt convention of dS_converged, whose (l,0) functions are the 4-pi-normalised Legendre functions to a relative 1.7e-9) and \(\dot\Lambda_{20}\) from TPWrate<N>, \(\dot J_2=-\sqrt5\,(\dot G_{20}-\dot\Lambda_{20}/g)/a\) (the centrifugal potential removed), and the TPW speed is \(|\dot m_1+i\,\dot m_2|\) rad/yr (times \(180/\pi\cdot10^6\) for degrees per Myr).

    They are the rates at \(t_N\) read from the modal states, not the mean rate over the last step that a difference (X<N> - X<N-1>) / dt gives. Use them with the second-order placement (RAMP=1, --ramp); with the default placement only the last increment is read as accruing linearly over its step and the history keeps the lag of the step placement, so the rates are then not more accurate than a two-point difference (Method).

    3. Scalar outputs¶

    quantity meaning units Fortran file Python
    ocean area area of C (ocean<N> = 1) m^2 ocean_area run_log.json scalars[N].ocean_area
    ice-free ocean area area of C * beta m^2 oceanBeta_area scalars[N].oceanBeta_area
    GMSL change over the ocean area-weighted mean of the sea-level change since step 0 over C (gmslc_ocn) m (rise positive) gmslc_ocnArea scalars[N].gmslc_ocn
    GMSL change over the ice-free ocean the same mean over C * beta (gmslc_ocnBeta) m gmslc_ocnBetaArea scalars[N].gmslc_ocnBeta
    ice volume integral of the ice thickness used as load (after the marine check when it is on, i.e. grounded ice only) m^3 of ice ice_volume scalars[N].ice_volume
    inner iterations iterations of the ocean-load update to reach epsilon1 at each step count numiter (one line per step 1..N) numiter.npy
    time k * dt since the start; with a times file t_k as given in it yr times (0, dt, 2 dt, ..., one line per step 0..N, ES14.4E2; with a times file t_0, t_1, ..., t_N in ES24.16E3) not written; run_log.json has dt_years, and with --times times_years (t_0..t_N); outer_log.json has times_years
    rotation see below TPW<N> not written
    rotation rates (optional) rates of the TPW<N> quantities, see above TPWrate<N> (with SLEVOLVE_RATES=1) TPWrate<N>.txt (with dSLdt or dRdt in --save-fields)

    The Fortran scalar files are text, one value per line (ES14.4E2), line 1 = step 0, line N+1 = step N (the GMSL files start with 0 at step 0). They are appended at every step and are written also in snapshot mode (SLEVOLVE_SNAP). Python stores the same quantities in run_log.json under scalars: entry 0 is step 0 (areas and ice volume only), entry N is step N. nmelt (Fortran) is the number of earlier load steps taken into account at each step, and elapsed_wall_time, elapsed_cpu_time are timings.

    TPW<N> (Fortran, rotation on): three records of the converged rotational quantities after step N (TPW0 = zeros), printed in Fortran column-major order:

    1. 9 values: il(3,3), the perturbation of the inertia tensor by the surface load (only il(1:3,3) is filled), kg m^2, physical: \(I_{i3}=-\int\sigma\,x_i x_3\,dA\) for the off-diagonal entries;
    2. 3 values: mm(1:3), the perturbation of the rotation vector, dimensionless: (m1, m2) is the displacement of the rotation pole in radians toward 0 E and 90 E (Mitrovica & Wahr 2011, eq. 10, \(m_1+i\,m_2\propto I_{13}+i\,I_{23}\): the pole moves away from added mass), m3 the relative change of the spin rate;
    3. 18 values: lambda(3,3), the degree-0 and degree-2 coefficients of the rotational driving potential in the U basis of dS_converged, as (real, imaginary) pairs, m^2 s^-2.

    All three are totals since step 0. m3 follows the sign of Mitrovica & Wahr (2011, eq. 15), \(m_3=-[(1+k^E_2)\,I_{33}+\text{viscous terms}]/C\): a load that lowers \(I_{33}\) (mass moved towards the axis) gives \(m_3>0\), a faster spin and a shorter day; the length-of-day change is \(-m_3\) times the length of the day. TPWrate<N> follows the same sign (correction of 2026-10-30, Release notes). The direction of true polar wander is atan2(m2-dot, m1-dot) of TPWrate<N>, in degrees east. The order-1 records (il13, il23, m1, m2, their rates) have the physical sign since the correction of 2026-10-31 (Release notes); directions read from earlier records are off by 180 degrees, while the fields, the TPW speed and \(\Lambda\) are unchanged.

    With the optional self-consistent axial rotation (AXIAL_ROTATION=deg2|full, --axial-rotation) the records keep their layout and meaning; m3 and its rate then include the rotational part of \(I_{33}\) (and, with full, the trace term), so \(\dot m_3\) and the rate of change of the length of day are about 0.4-0.5 % smaller in an ice age run and are consistent with \(\dot J_2\) from the geoid rate: \(-3C\dot m_3/(2Ma^2)\) (for full with \(C\dot m_3+\tfrac32\tau_c C\dot\Lambda_{00}/(a^2\Omega^2)\) in place of \(C\dot m_3\)) equals \(-\sqrt5\,(\dot G_{20}-\dot\Lambda_{20}/g)/a\) to about 1e-4 at the default inner tolerance, against about 0.4 % with the default equation, whose \(I_{33}\) has no rotational part.

    4. Outer iteration (ice age runs)¶

    file meaning
    tgrid0_<p> initial topography T0 used in outer pass p (m)
    pred_pres_topo_<p> topography at the last step of pass p, the predicted present topography (m)
    tgrid<k>_it<p>.npy Python run_outer.py: topography at step k of pass p, for the steps in --save-steps
    T0_next.npy Python: the initial topography for the next pass (Picard: tgrid0_<p> + (T_obs - pred_pres_topo_<p>); --outer secant uses the secant update)
    outer_log.json Python: per pass the misfit rms_pred_minus_obs (area-weighted RMS of pred_pres_topo_<p> - T_obs, m), max_abs, the inner iterations per step and the wall time
    numiter_<p> Fortran run_outer_mem: copy of numiter for pass p

    The Fortran files pred_pres_topo_<p>, tgrid0_<p> are written at the last step of each pass; in snapshot mode only when SLEVOLVE_OUTER is set.

    5. Which files each driver writes¶

    Fortran (OUTPUT_SLM/, .npy by default):

    • step 0: tgrid0, ocean0, beta0, dS_converged0, TPW0, and the first line of every scalar file;
    • each step N: tgrid<N>, ocean<N>, beta<N>, dS_converged<N>, R<N>, G<N>, TPW<N>, one more line in each scalar file;
    • with SLEVOLVE_SNAP=<list> only the listed steps (step 0 always) and the fields in SLEVOLVE_SNAP_FIELDS (default tgrid) are written as grids; snap_index.txt lists them. The Case 1 example (SLEVOLVE_SNAP=550 SLEVOLVE_SNAP_FIELDS=tgrid) writes tgrid0.npy and tgrid550.npy;
    • with SLEVOLVE_RATES=1: dSLdt<N>, dRdt<N> (snapshot fields dSLdt, dRdt) and TPWrate<N> at the steps where either rate grid is written.

    Python run_ais.py (--out):

    • step 0: tgrid0.npy, ocean0.npy, beta0.npy (always);
    • each step N: the fields named in --save-fields (default tgrid,ocean,beta; also R, G, dS, dSLdt, dRdt); dS is written as dS_converged<N>.npy; with dSLdt or dRdt and rotation also TPWrate<N>.txt;
    • at the end: numiter.npy, step_wall_seconds.npy, run_log.json (settings, timings, scalars).

    Python run_outer.py (--out): the files of §4, plus horiz<k>_it<p>.npy with --horizontal and dSLdt<k>_it<p>.npy, dRdt<k>_it<p>.npy, TPWrate<k>_it<p>.txt with --rates.

    To convert: python/tools/npy2text.py (text), python/tools/npy2nc.py (NetCDF with latitude, longitude and time axes; the time comes from OUTPUT_SLM/times when present, otherwise from step * --dt; for a Python run with non-uniform steps use the times_years of run_log.json).

    6. Files written, by solver¶

    Sections 1-4 define the meaning, sign, units and reference time of every output; this section is a short checklist of the files, by solver (section 5 gives the same per driver, with step detail).

    Fortran, per step N¶

    • Grids tgrid<N>, ocean<N>, beta<N>, R<N>, G<N> and the spectral field dS_converged<N>. By default every field is written at every step, as .npy (SLEVOLVE_FMT=npy, SLEVOLVE_BIN=1). Text copies are written only with SLEVOLVE_TEXT=1.
    • Restrict grid output with SLEVOLVE_SNAP / SLEVOLVE_SNAP_FIELDS. OUTPUT_SLM/snap_index.txt lists what was written.
    • Scalar text files, always written and appended per step: numiter, elapsed_wall_time, TPW<N>, area, GMSL and ice-volume files.

    Fortran, at the last step¶

    • pred_pres_topo_<k> and tgrid0_<k> (outer pass k).
    • These are not written in snapshot mode unless SLEVOLVE_OUTER is set.
    • run_outer_mem also copies numiter to numiter_<k> and prints == pass k wall … s.

    Optional horizontal displacement (off by default; Horizontal displacement output)

    • Fortran SLEVOLVE_HORIZ=1: horiz<N>.npy (spectral Psi_lm); SLEVOLVE_HORIZ=2: also Psi<N>.npy (Psi on the grid). Python --horizontal: horiz<N>.npy; --horizontal-grid: also utheta<N>.npy, uphi<N>.npy. Grid components for Fortran output: python/tools/horiz_grad.py horiz<N>.npy.

    Optional analytic rates (off by default; above)

    • Fortran SLEVOLVE_RATES=1: dSLdt<N>.npy, dRdt<N>.npy, TPWrate<N>. Python: dSLdt, dRdt in --save-fields (run_ais.py) or --rates (run_outer.py).

    Python¶

    • tgrid<N>.npy plus the fields in --save-fields (default tgrid,ocean,beta).
    • numiter.npy and run_log.json.
    • run_outer.py writes the outer-pass files as .npy, plus the steps listed in --save-steps.
