    Release notes and known limitations¶

    This page lists the contents of v1.0, its differences from the legacy code and its known limitations.

    v1.0¶

    Note of 2026-10-31: self-consistent axial rotation (optional extension). The axial Liouville equation of SLEvolve is that of Mitrovica & Wahr (2011, Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci. 39, 577-616, eq. 15), which leaves out the part of \(I_{33}\) driven by the change in centrifugal potential. A new option adds it: deg2 adds the rotational \(I_{33}\), the tidal \(k_2\) of the Love-number file acting on the solver's own \(\Lambda_{20}\) (the axial counterpart of Mitrovica & Wahr 2011, eqs. 13-14, and of the equatorial denominator \(1-k^T_2/k_f\) of Mitrovica et al. 2005, Geophys. J. Int. 161, 491-506, eq. 6); full also adds the degree-0 trace term. Fortran build switch AXIAL_ROTATION=off|deg2|full, Python --axial-rotation (run_ais.py, run_outer.py) and the keyword axial_rotation of the solver classes; the per-step solve and the analytic rate of \(m_3\) include the new terms, with the step and the ramp placement (Method). The default is off, and with it every result is identical to before. Size in the Case 3 ice age run: \(\dot m_3\) and the rate of change of the length of day 0.41 % smaller (deg2; 0.49 % with full), \(\dot J_2\) from the geoid 2e-5 relative, relative sea level at most 11 µm, TPW speed and direction below 1e-8 relative and 1e-6 degrees, run time unchanged within its noise (one scalar solve per inner iteration). With the option on, \(-3C\dot m_3/(2Ma^2)\) agrees with \(\dot J_2\) from the geoid (route gap of the two-route test 1.7e-9 instead of 0.485 %). Limitation: the trace term uses an elastic axial number (PREM, \(\tau_c=5.36\times10^{-4}\)), because the Love-number files have no degree-0 numbers; its viscous relaxation, about 3 % larger, would change \(m_3\) by at most 1.6e-5. Tests: tests/test_j2dot_two_routes.py gains the option-on cases, and the new tests/test_fortran_python_axial.sh compares the Fortran and Python solvers with the option on (Verification).

    Note of 2026-10-31: sign of the order-1 rotation records (correction). The spectral coefficients use the U basis, complex harmonics without the Condon-Shortley phase, whereas the expressions for the inertia products and the order-1 rotational potential (Milne & Mitrovica 1998, Geophys. J. Int. 133, 1-19, eqs. 33c and 43a,b) are written for harmonics with that phase (their eq. 33e). The inertia products il13, il23 and the coefficient \(\Lambda_{21}\) therefore each had the wrong sign. Both are corrected: il13, il23 are now \(I_{i3}=-\int\sigma\,x_i x_3\,dA\), and (m1, m2) is the physical displacement of the rotation pole toward 0 E and 90 E, with \(m_1+i\,m_2\propto I_{13}+i\,I_{23}\) (Mitrovica & Wahr 2011, Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci. 39, 577-616, eq. 10): added mass moves the pole away from itself. The error was inherited from the rotation block of the legacy code. Size of the change: the two signs cancelled, so no field, no \(\dot J_2\) and no TPW speed changes (in both solvers the topography, sea level, radial and horizontal displacement, rates and \(\Lambda\) are identical before and after); the sign of the order-1 rotation records il13, il23, m1, m2 in TPW<N> and of their rates in TPWrate<N> reverses, so directions of polar motion read from earlier records were off by 180 degrees. reference/ stores no rotation records and is unchanged. The legacy code has the same sign, so the round-off reproduction of the legacy code in the v1.0.0 paper is unaffected as a statement about that code. A new test, tests/test_tpw_direction.py, pins the direction (Verification). See Benchmark provenance.

    Note of 2026-10-30: sign of the axial rotation component m3 (correction). The axial component of the linearised Liouville equation used +1/C; the correct factor is -1/C, \(m_3=-[(1+k^E_2)\,I_{33}+\text{viscous terms}]/C\) (Mitrovica & Wahr 2011, Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci. 39, 577-616, eq. 15). A load that moves mass towards the rotation axis now speeds up the spin (\(m_3>0\)). The error was inherited from the rotation block of the legacy code. Both solvers are corrected, including the analytic rate of m3 in TPWrate<N>; the equatorial (polar-motion) equations and the rotational potential were already correct. Size of the change: \(\dot J_2\) about 1 % (Case 3 at the present, from the geoid rate: -7.139e-12 to -7.062e-12 /yr), the sign of the length-of-day rate reversed (Case 3: +0.124 to -0.124 ms per century), the polar-motion rate changed by a relative 2e-6, and relative sea level at the millimetre level (max |change| of the reference fields: Case 1 6.4 mm, Case 2 4.1 mm, Case 3 0.9 mm; 0.15 m on the 220 m of the 8-yr synthetic test case). With the correction, \(-3C\dot m_3/(2Ma^2)\) and \(\dot J_2\) read from the geoid agree in sign and to 0.4 %. reference/ and the expected outputs of the tests and of the quick start were recomputed; the documentation figures do not change visibly and were kept. The legacy code has the same sign, so the round-off reproduction of the legacy code in the v1.0.0 paper is unaffected as a statement about that code. See Benchmark provenance.

    Note of 2026-10-28: optional second-order load placement and analytic rates. Two opt-in additions, both off by default, so every default output, reference/ and the results of the paper are unchanged:

    • Second-order load placement (Fortran build switch RAMP=1, Python --ramp for run_ais.py and run_outer.py): each load increment accrues linearly over its step instead of being applied at its end. Each mode picks up the factor phi(s dt) = (1 - exp(-s dt)) / (s dt) in its memory and the same-step weight 1 - phi; the elastic and direct sea-level terms stay at the end of the step, and the polar-motion equation is solved in closed form with the implicit current increment. The time stepping becomes second order where the load is smooth in time; works with non-uniform steps, the extrapolated inner start and the warm start. See Method.
    • Analytic rates (Fortran SLEVOLVE_RATES=1, Python --save-fields ...,dSLdt,dRdt or run_outer.py --rates): the rates of sea level and radial displacement at the end of a step (dSLdt<N>, dRdt<N>, m/yr) and of the rotational quantities (TPWrate<N>), read from the modal states instead of a two-point difference; output only. Available in either build, but more accurate than a two-point difference only together with the ramp. See Output fields.

    The placement was derived and verified in a separate study. New tests: tests/test_ramp.py (in run_all.py) and tests/test_fortran_python_ramp.sh (Verifying the installation).

    Note of 2026-10-26: floating-ice convention. The ice function \(\beta\) is now the grounded-ice mask of Kendall et al. (2005, eqs 43-44): the ocean under floating ice shelves carries water load and belongs to the ocean area of the conservation term, and the flotation check uses the current topography iterate of the inner iteration (mask updated at most three times per step). The legacy code, and the code of the paper (Zenodo v1.0.0, doi 10.5281/zenodo.23422438), excluded every ice-covered cell, floating shelves included, from the water-loading ocean and tested flotation against the initial topography of the pass. The change applies to both solvers and has no switch; reference/, the expected outputs of the tests and the documentation figures were recomputed with it. New test: tests/test_floating_ice.py. See Benchmark provenance.

    Contents of the release

    • fortran/: the Fortran solver (src/), the build script build.sh with presets for the three benchmark cases, and the tools make_cache, text2npy and npy2text (tools/).
    • python/: the Python solver (run_ais.py forward run, run_outer.py ice age run with outer iteration, init_topo_segmented.py first-pass initial topography) and the converters and post-processing tools in python/tools/.
    • pyproject.toml: the Python solver as the pip-installable package slevolve 1.0.0 (sources in python/slevolve/, subpackage slevolve.tools; extras fast, plot, netcdf, all) with the console scripts slevolve-forward, slevolve-outer, slevolve-init-topo, slevolve-text2npy, slevolve-npy2text, slevolve-npy2nc. The scripts in python/ remain the no-install route.
    • tools/: input preparation for the benchmark cases (build_case1_total_ice.py, regrid.py, prepare_case2_inputs.sh, prepare_case3_nadi.py). No input data are redistributed.
    • lovenum/: the Love-number files of the three cases, in the degree-1 frame CE (k_1 = 0), and DEGREE1_NOTES.md on degree-1 Love numbers. Case 1 (prem_512.l60K2C.sum18p6.dum19p2.tz19p4.lm22) is the file distributed with Han et al. (2022), included unchanged; it is CE in its elastic and fluid limits but carries a degree-1 initialisation defect in its residues, which changes the degree-1 R, G and horizontal outputs but not sea level or topography. Cases 2 and 3 (prem_512.l120C.ump5.lm5, prem_512.l96C.ump3.lm1) were computed with the collocation method of Mitrovica and Peltier (1992) (after Peltier 1985).
    • tools/make_grid.py (grid files GLlat_512.txt, GLlon_512.txt) and python/tools/write_lovenum.py (Love numbers of your own Earth model in the solver's layout).
    • reference/: reference outputs of the three cases, checked with tests/check_reference.py.
    • tests/: the Python tests python tests/run_all.py (recursion vs explicit history sum, transform round trip, idealised small case, the extrapolated inner start for uniform and non-uniform steps, the small case with non-uniform steps), the Fortran test tests/test_fortran_python.sh (Fortran vs Python on a synthetic Earth), its non-uniform-step twin tests/test_fortran_python_vardt.sh (times file; also checks that a uniform times file reproduces the run without one exactly), the Fortran-only test tests/test_fortran_synthetic.sh (no Python; against reference/synthetic/tgrid8.npy) and tests/check_reference.py.
    • tools/make_synthetic_case.py and its Fortran twin tools/make_synthetic_case.f90 (synthetic-Earth input tree: an Earth-like random topography from a fixed seed, shipped as reference/synthetic/topography_seed.npy, and two melting Vialov ice sheets), python/tools/plot_fields.py (Mollweide and south polar stereographic maps with coastlines) and tools/plot_reference.py (maps of the reference results).
    • examples/: the quick start quickstart.py, run scripts for the three benchmark cases (case1_forward.sh, case2_one_pass.sh, case3_ice_age.sh, each for Fortran or Python) and the in-process outer-iteration wrapper run_outer_mem.sh.
    • docs/: this manual.

    Differences from the legacy code of Han et al. (2022)

    • The viscous history terms (sea level, radial displacement and the four degree-2 rotational terms) are carried in a recursive normal-mode state, advanced by one decay factor per step, instead of an explicit sum over the load history. The recursion is exact.
    • One process runs all time steps with the Earth model, modal state and transform tables resident; binary .npy input and output, with text <-> .npy converters in Fortran and Python.
    • Fewer spherical-harmonic transforms per step; Legendre transform by an on-the-fly recurrence or a BLAS table; FFTW; north-south symmetry; OpenMP.
    • Optional extrapolated start of the inner iteration and warm start of outer passes (these change only the starting point of an iteration; the converged result agrees within the inner tolerance).
    • Ice age runs: partially relaxed (segmented) first-pass initial topography, and an in-process outer-iteration driver.
    • Optional output of the horizontal surface displacement.
    • The longitude FFT routine fft99b of the transform module is used in a corrected form.

    The code lineage is described in Lineage and references.

    Known limitations

    • Ice-sheet coupling. The ice-sheet coupling interface inherited from the legacy code (the coupling flag and the coupled ice-input path in fortran/src/sl_model_mod.f90) is retained in the Fortran source but is not used by any benchmark case, is absent from the Python version, and has not been tested with the recursive state in v1.0.
    • Time steps. Uniform steps (the benchmark cases use 1 yr, 200 yr and 500 yr): Python --dt in years (run_ais.py, default 1; run_outer.py; init_topo_segmented.py), printed at start-up; Fortran compiled in by build.sh DT=<years> (the forward driver run_from_zero_plus uses the build-time DT, verified against Python with DT=1 and DT=5). Non-uniform steps: an optional times file, one time in years per ice file (format), read by Fortran at run time from INPUT_FILES/others/times or SLEVOLVE_TIMES=<path> (build with DT unset) and by Python with --times <file>. The benchmark cases use uniform steps; the non-uniform mode is verified on the idealised small case and the synthetic Earth (tests/test_small_case_vardt.py, tests/test_fortran_python_vardt.sh), not on a benchmark case.

    What the times file changes (step j has length dt_j = t_j - t_(j-1)):

    1. the decay factors of the modal-state update, `d_k = exp(-s_k dt_j)`, recomputed whenever the
   step length changes (cached otherwise), in both solvers; the recursion remains exact for the
   step-wise load history (`tests/test_recursion.py`, `tests/test_small_case_vardt.py`);
2. the extrapolated inner start: from step 3 on,
   `dS0_j = dt_j [Sdot_(j-1) + (Sdot_(j-1) - Sdot_(j-2)) (dt_j + dt_(j-1)) / (dt_(j-1) + dt_(j-2))]`,
   `Sdot_i = dS_i / dt_i`, which is exactly `2 dS_(j-1) - dS_(j-2)` for equal steps
   (`tests/test_extrap_formula.py`);
3. the time stamps: Fortran `OUTPUT_SLM/times` holds `t_0 .. t_N` (written as `ES24.16E3` instead of
   the uniform-step `ES14.4E2`, so that non-integer times are kept); Python `run_log.json` and
   `outer_log.json` store `times_years` (`run_log.json` `dt_years` = `"variable"`);
4. Python `init_topo_segmented.py --times`: the segment ages are `t_N - t_n` instead of `dt (N - n)`.

    Unchanged: the step indexing (ice file k is read at step k; the Fortran NSTEPS, L_sim/dt1, the time window TIMEWINDOW, the checkpoint cadence and the --save-steps/SLEVOLVE_SNAP step numbers count steps, not years). Without a times file the code paths are those of uniform steps, and a uniform times file reproduces them exactly. The legacy input_times/timearray flag of user_specs_mod is not used (it still stops the exact-memory path when set). * Fortran grid and names. nglv is fixed at 512, and the input file names are compiled in: every new case needs a rebuild. * Love numbers are read, not computed (Love numbers). * Python defaults. run_ais.py has development defaults for --earth, --icedir, --topo and --out, and run_outer.py the Case 3 defaults --icepfx nadi_ --nfiles 51 --dt 500; always pass them explicitly. * Converters. Self-describing metadata (grid, units, time axis) exist only via npy2nc.py. The checkpoint and reuse_<k>.bin record files are not converted to .npy. * Build portability. Only the macOS / Apple-silicon configuration of Installation has been tested; the non-conda (FFTW_PREFIX) and OpenBLAS paths have not. * Anderson mixing, the secant outer update and the --reuse variants other than warm are experimental and were not used in the paper. * The example scripts in examples/ are templates for the benchmark cases; they expect the input trees produced by the scripts in tools/.

    Checks made during development¶

    These checks were made during development with tools that are not part of this release; they are recorded here and are not meant to be repeated.

    Exactness against the legacy solver (code of the paper, Zenodo v1.0.0, before the floating-ice correction of 2026-10-26)

    • Run both solvers on the same inputs and configuration with SLEVOLVE_INNER=off and no warm start. The legacy reference build uses the corrected fft99b. The legacy build scripts and the comparison scripts used during development are not part of this release.
    • Pass criteria:
    • final topography differs by < 1e-11 m between exact SLEvolve variants and the legacy solver (binary output);
    • identical numiter at every step;
    • identical ocean and beta masks.
    • Comparisons against legacy text output are limited by the ES16.9E2 print precision (~4e-6 m).

    Acceptance criteria used during development

    • Every stage was accepted only after it reproduced the preceding stage numerically on the benchmark cases (paper Sect. 2.8): same inner-iteration counts and masks, and topography within round-off.
    • Switches that only change the iteration start (extrap, warm, T0 guess) were accepted on agreement within epsilon1 and on the converged outer misfit.
    • Fortran and Python are cross-checked against each other.
    • Repeat runs give identical output.

    File converters

    • Converters, on one file of each text layout (row-e.18, row-e16.7, row-E18.10):
    • Fortran- and Python-written .npy files are identical.
    • np.load of the Fortran file equals the text parse.
    • text -> npy -> text reproduces the original file exactly in its own format, in both languages.
    • es16.9 output is identical between Fortran and Python and round-trips exactly.
    • The Fortran reader also handles fortran_order=True and |i1 files.
    • Solver, 20 Case 1 steps with all snapshot fields: reading <f4 .npy ice and a <f8 .npy topography gives output identical to reading the headerless .bin caches, and all scalar text outputs are identical (except CPU time).
    • Python, 20 steps, lmax 511: reading the Fortran-written .npy inputs gives output identical to reading the text files; the .npy headers of tgrid, ocean and dS_converged are identical to the Fortran files'.
